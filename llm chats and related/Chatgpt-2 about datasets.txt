## 1. 🥇 Best starting dataset: Food-Pack-Mapper

A **2026 open-access study** specifically created a dataset to map food-packaging materials using **OTR and WVTR**, which is almost exactly what SIH26236 asks for.

The study collected permeability data from **49 studies** and produced **295 permeability data points**, covering materials including **LDPE, HDPE, PLA, gelatin-based films and starch/gelatin/CMC blends**. It standardized OTR to `cm³/m²/day` and WVTR to `g/m²/day`. ([Nature][1])

Even better, the authors provide the processed dataset and code through GitHub:

[Food-Pack-Mapper dataset & code](https://github.com/whps0620/Food-Pack-Mapper/tree/main?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

This should be **one of our core datasets**.

# 2. 🥈 CIRAD / Dataverse food-packaging dataset

There is another very useful open dataset created specifically around **food packaging composition and gas permeability**.

It was extracted from **50 scientific articles**, with annotation performed independently by three experts. It contains quantitative and symbolic information about packaging composition and permeability. ([ScienceDirect][2])

The newer Food-Pack-Mapper study also identifies this as its raw-data source. ([Nature][3])

[CIRAD Dataverse — Food packaging dataset](https://dataverse.cirad.fr/dataset.xhtml?persistentId=doi%3A10.18167%2FDVN1%2FU7HK8J&utm_source=chatgpt.com)

This is particularly valuable because it gives us **scientific literature-derived data**, rather than manufacturer marketing claims.

[NIFTEM-T — Packaging Resin Properties Handbook](https://niftem-t.ac.in/olapp/pmfme/upload/mt_handbook_spice.pdf?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

# 7. The important issue: DON'T simply combine the numbers

This is extremely important for our AI model.

Look at these examples:

**OTR might be reported at:**

`23°C / 50% RH`

while another source reports:

`25°C / 0% RH`

and another may use a completely different test method.

Those numbers **cannot simply be placed into one column and treated as equivalent**.

The 2026 Food-Pack-Mapper study specifically had to standardize units and conditions before performing its clustering analysis. ([Nature][1])

So our dataset needs columns like:

```text
Material
Material Type
Structure
Thickness
OTR
OTR Unit
OTR Temperature
OTR RH
WVTR
WVTR Unit
WVTR Temperature
WVTR RH
Test Method
Source
DOI
Year
```

# 10. We can make a third dataset: Food → Packaging requirements

This would be the **most valuable dataset** for our AI.

For example:

| Food                   | Main Concern                | Required Barrier           | Packaging Type        |
| ---------------------- | --------------------------- | -------------------------- | --------------------- |
| Potato chips           | Oxidation + moisture        | High O₂ + moisture barrier | High-barrier laminate |
| Fresh leafy vegetables | Respiration                 | Controlled gas exchange    | Breathable film       |
| Frozen vegetables      | Moisture/freezer protection | Moisture barrier           | Suitable freezer film |
| Biscuits               | Moisture pickup             | High moisture barrier      | Barrier film          |
| Spices                 | Moisture + oxidation        | High barrier               | High-barrier laminate |

**But these food-to-packaging relationships must be backed by scientific/industry sources.** We should not generate them ourselves and call them ground truth.

---

# 11. A particularly interesting new source

The 2026 **npj Science of Food** paper is almost a proof-of-concept for what we're trying to build.

It used OTR and WVTR data and applied:

* K-Means
* Gaussian Mixture Models
* DBSCAN

to cluster sustainable packaging materials according to barrier properties. DBSCAN achieved a reported silhouette score of **0.910** in their experiment. ([Nature][3])

That means we could potentially say in our SIH presentation:

> "Our recommendation engine uses experimentally reported packaging-property data and a data-driven similarity/recommendation approach."

rather than claiming that we trained an AI model from random synthetic data.

## 🚨 One thing I would NOT do

Don't create a dataset like:

```text
LDPE → good for chips
PET → good for biscuits
HDPE → good for milk
```

and then train Random Forest on it.

That's too simplistic and judges can easily ask:

> **"What is your ground truth?"**

Instead, our system should be scientifically defensible:

```text
Scientific literature
        ↓
Extract raw measurements
        ↓
Normalize units + conditions
        ↓
Material-property database
        ↓
Food requirement database
        ↓
Recommendation rules/model
        ↓
Explainable recommendation
```

That gives us a much stronger SIH project.
